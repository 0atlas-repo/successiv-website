## What changed

An outside reviewer sent back a rewrite of the Capabilities page. Checked every
claim in it against the repositories behind the site, agreed with two findings,
and found one they missed.

**Withdrew capability 07, "Retail operations that scale."** It claimed
"storefront and back-of-house tooling for teams running more than one location
or channel." Nothing supports it:

- The retail / mall commerce ops *work* entry was dropped on 2026-09-17 for
  having no repository. The capability line survived that pass untouched.
- Shopmgr, the only commerce product shipping, says the opposite in its own
  copy: "It is a front-of-house assistant, not a back-office system", and it
  "does not touch inventory, payments, or fulfilment". One Shopify store, not
  several locations.

Traced the source: `docs/BRIEF.md` lists six capabilities, including
"E-commerce / retail ops" and "Document & signature workflows". Both withdrawn
claims came from that list, and both reached the live site. The brief's
capabilities section now carries a superseded banner saying so.

**Fixed the count.** The page lede said "Six areas we've shipped into
production more than once" while eight shipped. The lede was hardcoded in
`src/pages/capabilities.astro`, not in content, which is how it drifted. It now
lives in `capabilities.ts` as `capabilitiesPage` and carries no number, so
adding or withdrawing an entry cannot make it lie again.

**Softened two claims that overreached:**

- 06 said "Tender, contract, and *operations portals*". The ops work is an
  assistant answering from an indexed procedure library; it is not a portal,
  and `work.ts` says it deliberately does not decide who owns an incident. Now
  "Tender, contract, and recognition portals" — awards portals are real and do
  carry the states, owners, and audit trail the line claims.
- 07 (was 08) claimed "every piece has a state and an owner anyone can see".
  The CMS has states and a timestamped backup trail, but explicitly no
  approval chains and no per-section editor roles, so no owner. Rewritten
  around what it does: one record holds every language version, preview before
  publish, publishing as a status change rather than a developer ticket.

The homepage proof strip renders the same list, and seven divides into neither
two nor four columns, so the last cell spans two and closes the row.

## What the reviewer got right, and what their method cannot catch

Right: the six-versus-eight contradiction, and the fix — drop the number rather
than correct it. Their prose was tighter than ours in places.

Their defence was "I did not invent claims, only tightened what's already on
your site." True, and that is the hole. Tightening cannot catch a claim the
site should never have carried. Retail survives their rewrite intact, and their
new opening — "if your problem sits in one of these areas, we have likely met
it in production before" — would have extended the production claim to cover it.

## Mock realism — pilot (branch `mock-realism`)

Founder on the mocked screens: *"at this point they look like wireframe."*

Read the 39 files before proposing anything. The frame was not the problem —
`.mock-frame` already carries a per-theme drop shadow and a cyan wash. Five
things inside it were:

1. Drawn at 9–10.5px, which is diagram scale, not the 13–14px of a real
   screenshot reduced.
2. `MockFrame` was three dots and a title. No rail, no toolbar.
3. Every row identical — no selected row, no hover, no focus.
4. Monochrome. `global.css` had no `ok`/`warn`/`danger` token at all, so a
   status pill was impossible without a raw hex, which light forbids.
5. Three rows, no avatars, nothing running off the bottom edge.

Spec and plan: `local_pm/projects/mock-realism/`. Pilot only — tokens, the
shell, and two screens. The other 37 are untouched pending founder review.

### What shipped in the pilot

- **Status tokens.** `--color-ok/-warn/-danger` plus a `-soft` companion, in
  `@theme` and both dark blocks. Contrast computed, not eyeballed: light
  5.4–6.5:1 on the page and 4.6–5.4:1 on its own tint; dark 8.5–11.6:1 and
  6.8–8.5:1. The dark `-soft` mixes are deliberately unequal (ok 16%, warn 13%,
  danger 15%) because at one percentage the yellow reads as a slab.
- **`MockFrame` shell.** Nav rail, toolbar, content pane on its own ground, and
  a signed-in avatar. No new props, so all 39 screens pick it up unchanged.
- **`ChatMock`** and **`CreatorMock`** rebuilt at UI scale.

### Three defects the browser found that the code review would not have

- **The product card cropped the new chrome.** Its window is a fixed 268px with
  the mock centred; at 521px tall that centring cut off the title bar and
  toolbar and showed a headless list. `ProductCard` now anchors the mock to the
  top, so the window keeps its chrome and clips at the bottom instead — which is
  the cropped-screenshot look the whole change was after.
- The send glyph pointed left.
- At 390px the "In stock" pill wrapped onto two lines.

### Claim discipline

Nothing gained a capability. The chat screen shows a grounded catalogue lookup
and an escalation that raises a typed ticket, both of which Shopmgr's own copy
already stands behind; the gig screen shows the pending/available split the
product actually has, and still says nothing about settlement. No KPI tiles —
the row of big aggregate numbers a "dashboard look" reaches for is exactly the
invented metric the brief forbids.

Judgment call for the founder to overturn if they disagree: the toolbar has a
search field and a filter chip on every screen, including screens whose product
may not have search. Read as generic app chrome rather than a named capability.
One line in `MockFrame.astro` removes it.
